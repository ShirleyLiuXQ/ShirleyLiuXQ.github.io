## Academic:
I am a postdoctoral researcher at Oxford Statistics working 
with [Prof. Patrick Rebeschini](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~rebeschi/). My research aims to develop <span style="color:darkred">statistical and information-theoretic foundations</span> that make machine learning systems more efficient and reliable, especially when data is heterogeneous, high-dimensional, or changing over time. 

Empirical progress in machine learning continues to outpace our theoretical understanding of it. I work on the slow side of that gap: characterising what is achievable in principle, and designing systems and algorithms that approach those limits.

<p style="margin-bottom: 0;">I have primarily been working on three problems recently:</p>
<ul style="margin-top: 0;">
<li>Mathematically understand common heuristics and phenomena in <b>model training</b>, such as early stopping of gradient descent, benign overfitting, and feature learning</li>  
<li><b>Sequential decision-making</b> on heterogeneous data with rigorous guarantees, including in bandits and adaptive inference</li>
<li><b>Uncertainty quantification</b> under minimal assumptions, using e-values, conformal prediction, and prediction-powered inference</li>
</ul>
 
I completed my PhD at Cambridge, where I was fortunate to be advised by [Prof. Ramji Venkataramanan](https://rv285.github.io/). My PhD work centered on <span style="color:darkred">information theory</span> and <span style="color:darkred">high-dimensional statistics</span>. Prior to my PhD, I obtained my BA and MEng in Information and Computer Engineering from Cambridge.

I was an intern with 
**Microsoft Research Cambridge** in 2023, where I designed error correction codes for Project Silica, a novel glass-based archival data storage technology. This work has recently appeared in [*Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10042-w).
